About the game

Every level starts as a picture

Pixel Fuse is a falling-block puzzle where every level starts as a full picture built from colored obstacle blocks — a turtle, a whale, a rocket, a Chinese character. Your job is to clear the entire board. Guide falling pieces into place and complete a line to clear it.

The signature twist: bombs are baked into the picture itself. Detonate one and it clears a wide strip along its own row; fuse four bombs into a square and it clears several rows the full width of the board. Chain them together to clear the board faster.

How to play

Learn it in a minute

Objective

Clear the whole board. Guide each falling piece left, right, and down; complete a full line to clear it, and detonate the bombs baked into the picture for bigger clears.

Controls

⌗
On-screen buttons
Left, right, rotate, soft drop, and hard drop as dedicated on-screen buttons — the default control scheme.
✛
D-Pad
Prefer a single directional pad instead? Switch to D-Pad mode anytime in Settings.
↺
Undo
Up to 2 undos per level. One undo fully rewinds your last move.
💣
Bombs
Baked into the picture itself. Clear them like any other block, or set them off for a bigger blast radius.

Star rating

★☆☆Clear the level.
★★☆Clear it without using undo.
★★★Clear it at or above the level's target score.
Strategy

Tips & tricks

  1. Watch how bombs are clustered. A lone bomb only clears a strip along its row; fusing four into a square instead clears several full-width rows.
  2. Undo is for experiments, not just mistakes. Two undos per level fully restores the board, so use one to try a risky placement and see what happens.
  3. A full line always clears, bomb or not. Sometimes the fastest path through a tricky board is a plain completed line, not a detonation.
  4. Plan for where a piece lands, not just where you're dropping it from. Rotate before you commit — once a piece locks in, it's part of the board.
  5. Not your style? Switch it up. Buttons and D-Pad controls are both free to try anytime in Settings.
  6. Save your undos for the picture's trickiest corners. A single undo can be the difference between a clean 3-star clear and starting over.
Questions

FAQ

Is Pixel Fuse free to play? ▶
Check the App Store listing for the current price. There are no in-app purchases, no ads, and no subscriptions — you get the complete game with every level.
Does Pixel Fuse collect my data? ▶
No. Pixel Fuse has no accounts, no analytics SDKs, no ad frameworks, and no network access of any kind — not even iCloud sync. Your progress (stars, best scores, unlocked stages) is stored locally on your device only, and never leaves it.
What's the difference between bomb blast types? ▶
A single bomb clears a 7-wide strip along its own row. Four bombs fused into a 2×2 square instead clear several rows, the full width of the board.
Can I switch between on-screen buttons and D-Pad? ▶
Yes — it's a toggle in Settings, and it applies the next time you're in a level.
What happens if the board fills up? ▶
The level ends and you can Retry immediately or head back to Level Select — no penalty beyond starting the level over.
Does it work on iPad? ▶
Yes, full iPhone and iPad support, including both iPad orientations.
What iOS version is required? ▶
Pixel Fuse requires iOS 18.0 or later.
What languages does Pixel Fuse support? ▶
English, German, French, Spanish, and Chinese. Pixel Fuse follows your device's language setting automatically.

Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 2026 · Published by Frank Neumann

What we collect

Nothing. Pixel Fuse has no user accounts, no login, and no registration of any kind. The app includes no analytics SDKs, no advertising frameworks, no crash reporters, and no third-party tracking code — only first-party Apple frameworks (SpriteKit, SwiftUI, Foundation). Pixel Fuse makes no network requests of any kind, and has no backend server.

Your game progress

Which levels you've cleared, your stars, your best scores, and your settings are stored locally on your device only, using Apple's standard UserDefaults storage. This data never leaves your device — it isn't synced anywhere, and it's never transmitted to the developer or any third party.
